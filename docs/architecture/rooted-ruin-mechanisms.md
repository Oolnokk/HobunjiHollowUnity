# Rooted ruin mechanisms

The live-growth preview and playable ruin share `LiveRootMechanisms`. The playable adapter opts in with `DevRandomRuin.generate(seed, { rootMechanisms: true })`, the `generation.rootMechanisms` config field, or **Rooted mechanisms** beside the Dev Settings Generate button. Ordinary ruins retain their existing presentation.

## Progress and ownership

`DevRandomRuin` owns puzzle signals, manual controls, external overrides, locks and progress timing. Its existing DynamicSurfaces frame client calls V50 `applyProgress`; a bound mechanism forwards that normalized progress to its rooted controller. Hidden native geometry still updates logical child anchors for glyphs/effects. It provides neither the rendered mechanism nor walkable support. Native dais rider carrying is disabled for bound mechanisms.

V50 captures the actual generated object, not a generic replacement preset. World-space poses are rebased around a stationary anchor; nonuniform transforms are baked into each guide. Native transforms and linked signal values are restored after capture. Each unique pose owns a root growth, invariant pieces share one growth, and repeated poses are holds. Material-only decals/effects retain their native presentation.

## Collision

Playable transitions use overlapping growth weights: incoming roots grow fully before outgoing roots finish retracting. The preview's ordinary linear cross-growth remains available. DynamicSurfaces uses fixed per-piece keyframe envelopes, enabled at 85% local part growth. Envelopes are captured once; no per-frame mesh traversal or bounds reconstruction is added. Dais payloads and shafts do not become walkable decks.

Lifts and stairs capture intermediate poses at at most 0.25 world units of vertical extent change. These fixed growth steps fit below the existing 0.42 step limit. No invisible moving deck carries riders. Mechanisms requiring more than 16 safe poses fail preparation explicitly.

Doors retain the single tile/footprint collision authority. Their binding reports whether the closed shape has significant local growth; hidden guide materials do not invalidate its semantic footprint. Closing pauses while the player's footprint overlaps the doorway. Camera occlusion uses actual grown meshes and their changing draw ranges instead of hidden guides.

## Lifecycle and checks

Preparation happens after the normal native solvability audit and before entering the map. Failed/cancelled builds dispose captured guides, generated roots and surface records. Reroll/exit abort preparation and release all published bindings. Native materials are restored exactly once; source meshes and their geometry remain owned by V50.

`test-live-root-mechanisms.js` covers growth weights, support overlap, fixed supports, occupied-door closing, disposal and cancellation. `test-root-mechanism-runtime-browser.js` runs actual Three.js, V50 capture, root geometry, DynamicSurfaces and semantic door footprints. It checks linked-signal restoration, lift support continuity, step clearance and payload exclusion.


## Full Stone Age mode

**Rooted Stone Age ruin** in the Dev Settings Generate row now selects both the existing rooted mechanism adapter and the whole Stone Age presentation. Use DevRandomRuin.generate(seed, { stoneAge: true, rootMechanisms: true }) to request it programmatically. Ordinary wilderness ruins and explicit stoneAge:false generations preserve the original Harlyao furniture and flooring. A rooted Stone Age generation uses the native V50 configured multi-room/hallway layout, the nine optional Stone Age architectural recipes and pillars-only floor props.

StoneAgeRuinScenery is shared with the standalone author preview. The game presents the same farm-cliff-textured rockified walls/pillars, quarter-scale cell-masked wilderness boulder floor and permanent Temple root/vine layers before applying native rooted mechanism keyframes. Stone Age pieces retain V50 placement; rock conversion is visual-only and does not mutate the immutable V50 source or original furniture guides. The quarter-scale floor is a visual skin: V50 floor cells, plateau heights, entrances and DynamicSurfaces remain the sole navigation/step authority, avoiding incorrect collision with decorative boulder crests. The shared author preview still displays its 4 × 4 repeated *single-room* floor; playable multi-room generation instead masks the stone mesh exactly to the generator's floor-cell set, using local quarter-scale Voronoi boulders (including 6.25% source relief) at the runtime's per-cell elevations. Disconnected rooms, one-cell corridors and floor holes do not receive phantom stone, and the native V50 room/puzzle graph and mixed-height tiers remain intact.

The scenery build is abortable. Original V50 wall/pillar meshes retain their visible/material authority for static SolidFootprints and puzzle collision; only their main-camera rendering layer is suppressed, and exact layer masks are restored on exit, reroll or failure before V50 reclaims its preview root. Generated roots and buffers are disposed. Its boulder generation passes private elevation dependencies to ZoneTerrainFeatures.buildUndiggableBoulderShellData rather than invoking ZoneTerrainFeatures.init, which would corrupt the live wilderness scene. Diagnostics include the active mode, piece set, room count, walls, pillars, masked floor-cell/void counts, distinct elevation levels, boulder count and visible strands; the mobile Generate button reports vegetation preparation.

The game page loads NaturalSurfaceMaterials and the furniture stretch-UV mapper on demand, only after opting into this mode; these author tools are not loaded or monkey-patched during regular Harlyao generation. A failed script fetch appears as a generated-ruin error instead of rendering untextured stone.

### Floor generation performance

Playable multi-room floors request **two subdivisions per source cell** from `ZoneTerrainFeatures.buildUndiggableBoulderShellData`. The wilderness generator and standalone author preview keep their unchanged four-subdivision default. Previously, the playable path constructed four subdivisions and then discarded 75% of the top triangles via `compactFloorStoneIndices` before rendering. The new low-detail path bypasses that discard pass. The deterministic regression compares the complete final triangle coordinates from both paths; the rendered boulder shapes, door gaps, sunken tiers and underlying V50 collision are unchanged.

In a same-runtime JavaScript microbenchmark with seeded 480-, 2,073- and 4,236-cell floors, shell construction measured respectively **99→36 ms**, **412→152 ms** and **856→296 ms** (2.7–2.9× faster; hardware/runtime dependent). Source triangle counts fall by 75%, and temporary source vertex counts fall by roughly two-thirds. Total loading also includes wall rockification, UV mapping and Temple foliage generation, which these measurements do not cover. Inspect `DevRandomRuin` Stone Age diagnostics' `floorBuildMs` to compare whole floor geometry/UV generation on an actual phone. Never use this optimization for wilderness boulders, whose 4×4 surface grid remains authoritative.


## Natural root snare hands — author-only two-grip encounter

The Root Growth Author's \`tools/live-root-growth/index.html\` remains the sole owner of this experimental interaction; playable dungeons do **not** spawn these hands yet. The preview now loads actual NPC records from \`config/npcs/hobunji-starter-npc-database.json\`. It uses the **Animation Author's portrait pipeline** (\`NpcAvatarPreview.buildProfileFromNpcExport\`, front and behind portrait rendering, \`PNGPlaneAvatar.buildSinglePlaneAvatarModel\` with a skinned neck), the same species-specific \`ProceduralLegAnimation.attach\`/\`LegBones\` runtime, and \`PantsGarmentRenderer.attachToLegs\` for equipped dyed trousers or skirts. No simplified primitive replacement is displayed when this loading fails; an on-screen error identifies the missing asset/runtime. A repository NPC selector and live rig metadata appear in the author UI. Every hiding place is a deterministic, fixed node on a real wall- or floor-root strand, seeded once per generated ruin. Two finger branches and an opposing thumb grow from one small fork at the end of the steerable branch arm, using the real foliage-generated bark/wonk geometry.

**Current default tuning:** notice radius **15 units**, maximum arm reach **15 units**, arm extension progress **0.2/second** and walker speed **5 world units/second**. The arm continuously retargets its joints to the runner's changing actual neck/feet point as it grows; an animated open palm and a sphere collider, *not* just the detection distance, determine first contact. The defaults and any author edits are included in exported recipes.

**First grip — neck tug:** The first colliding root claims the neck. The runner continues receiving movement commands, and its input movement direction is compared with the horizontal direction back to that root's **fixed** node. The movement multiplier is \`1.25 + 0.75 * dot(inputDirection, towardNode)\`: exactly **0.5× when running away**, **2× when running toward the root**, and **1.25× for perpendicular motion**. A small directional steering term makes it feel like a tether, but cannot prevent walking. The gripping branch tracks the actual neck point while the runner continues its procedural gait.

**Second grip — feet stretch and split:** A distinct branch may then grip the lower leg/feet point. This immobilizes the preview walker, and both root hands track their respective **real skinned neck bone and leg-foot anchors**. The character lifts for 0.55 seconds. **Its entire portrait, clothing, trousers and articulated feet then tilt together** so the body axis follows the relative positions of the neck-gripping and feet-gripping hands; both root arms aim at the matching neck/feet world-space grip targets. The upper portrait and the actual skinned pants+procedural-feet rig then separate over **three seconds** along that moving axis. The bridging stretch surface samples the NPC's own waist pixels rather than an unrelated wooden cylinder. At the end, the stretching connector disappears, the pants and feet remain separately attached to their hand and a short burst of red stylized droplets sprays from the separation. Both hands continue holding their respective pieces until explicit reset; the user can also force a reproducible two-hand preview via **Preview two-hand neck + feet stretch**. **Reset / release runner** restores the original rig, movement, anchor grip state and droplets. This scene does not affect real game characters, collision, health, drops or saved world state.

The author exposes mobile-friendly controls for reveal nodes, source-relative run-toward/run-away, random runner direction, reach and growth rates. Diagnostic fields report neck/feet grip ids, tug multiplier, dual-grip progression, stretch time, separation count and source node identity. \`scripts/test-root-snare-author-browser.js\` tests the real three.js instance, including natural first/second collider captures, exact dot-product speed multipliers, 3-second stretch, burst generation, manual reset, and the unchanged 16-region Temple floor root guide parity. \`scripts/test-root-snare-author-integration.js\` checks that the mobile UI, versioned modules, exported recipes and gait integration stay wired.

**Author/playable floor vegetation parity:** The Root Growth Author repeats one Temple cover across sixteen quarter-size stone tiles. \`StoneAgeRuinScenery.buildFloorCoverSectors\` partitions native playable V50 floors into up to sixteen disjoint, void/elevation-preserving regions with the same Temple-derived root/vine layers and per-region budgets. Cloned guide ownership is remapped and an invisible floor geometry reference remains for existing growth planning; no extra gameplay collision surface or full-floor duplicate appears.
