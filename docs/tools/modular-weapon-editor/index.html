04 · Rigid fitting lines and rope mounts



Heads and hafts require matching tang or eye lines to assemble.




For ordinary rigid pairs, tang/eye arrows determine fitting, rotation and length-axis stretch. When a rigid head hangs from a flail shaft, its cyan rope contact is edited separately in section 02; the shared rigid head arrow is protected. A flail head ignores its modular head arrow entirely: Complex Tool Visuals supplies its attachment point. Purple dashed haft connector guides indicate the physical rope start and the mounting-line extension. Gold lines are tangs and aqua lines eyes; all coordinates use the original 450 × 1204 sprite frame.









In Draw mode, drag base to arrow tip. In Move mode, drag the crosshair to shift the whole line. In Rotate mode, drag the circle on its short arm to turn the line around its midpoint without changing its length.







Line length is the distance from its fixed origin to its arrow tip. For a rope line, this is the full hanging connector reach only; changing it never scales the head or changes rope/chain thickness. Use the number box, its native arrows, or the − / + buttons.





A haft may have tang, eye and rope lines. The rope line is the exact attachment and connector reach whenever present. Without one, the flail uses the exact attachment authored in Complex Tool Visuals — never a modular-head fitting line. Explicit exclusions still take precedence.

07 · Metalwork, brushes and recipe boxes

Click Auto-bridge gaps to join a head that floats off its haft automatically, or paint fat strokes on the assembled weapon yourself; each stroke applies as soon as you let go. The game re-runs the same hollow-and-fill once when it builds this exact combination (cached, never per frame). Metal under strokes is kept; only empty space and outlines are regrown.






Recipe boxes. Copy a manual process into a scaled box. Drag on the preview to draw a box around the work (say, one end of the haft), name it and Save recipe. On any pair, Place recipe box puts the recipe in a box (sized from that pair’s head), and every time you move or resize the box (drag its edges and corners) the recipe is re-mapped into it and re-run. Place on every eligible pair only offers multi-ended hafts with at least two distinct tang positions and an eye mount, after showing a confirmation. Kylie and other one-ended hafts are never included.






















Press, drag to position (a ghost follows), release to place. Then bridge, smudge or Auto-bridge it to the parts on either side.















Clean up outline pulls black out of the metal (the outline’s dark fringe smeared in by smudging, and enclosed black specks up to the speck size become clean metal), then strips the outline around the metal, smooths its edge (dents and bumps up to the smoothing size) and redraws it at one even thickness measured from the sprite. The button works everywhere your brush work changed the image, leaving untouched artwork exactly as drawn; the Clean outline brush does the same wherever you paint, original artwork included.






Both read the pixels and average them broadly, only where your brush work changed the image. Even out outline wobble measures how far the outline wanders from its own broad average, then pulls bumps in and dents out toward that average, picking the averaging size automatically (the slider is how much of the achievable calm-down to take) and redrawing the outline at one even thickness. Clean up to my style reads your untouched art for its tones and smallest tone pool, then flattens small texture away: every metal pixel becomes exactly the shaded or unshaded color, black outline pure black, no half-transparent pixels, tone patches smaller than the smallest pool merge into the tone around them, then the outline is evened as above.




Separate shading finds patches where shaded and unshaded metal are mixed together (more than the tolerance of the other tone nearby) and swaps their pixels so each tone gathers into larger pools (about the pool size across). Tones keep their exact colors and counts. The revert brush eases back toward the image as it was before your first smudge-type stroke, or to wherever you last pressed Set revert point (heal with big strokes, set the point, then revert only the fiddly small ones); the plow’s blade is twice the brush radius wide.






White-highlighted arrow is the selected haft line. Move and Rotate gizmos act on the whole selected line, while leaving other lines untouched. The first arrow point is the mounting base, and the arrow tip is the forward direction. Matching directed lines determine position and orientation. Multi-ended hafts may have multiple lines of either kind.

Smudge metal pushes metal along with the brush (nothing else moves) and, on release, strips the outline around what changed and regenerates it at the sprite’s own measured thickness. Regenerate metal outline does the same for the whole sprite.


Bridge brush: paint a fat stroke from one metal piece to another and it fills as soon as you let go (Undo reverts it). Auto-bridge gaps does this for you, joining every separate piece with the shortest strokes (brush radius sets their width). Metal under the stroke is kept, the rest keeps a black rim wherever it meets empty space, separate unshaded pools touching it get joined by an unshaded channel, and every metal pixel touching it races to grow its shaded or unshaded tone inward, carrying the parts’ shading bands into the bridge. A shaded band runs alongside every black outline: the set percentage of the bridge’s width on the side facing away from the light, thinning to a sliver on the lit side. Only pixels in the base metal color seed the fill; other colors under the stroke are kept as-is. Undo works here too.
